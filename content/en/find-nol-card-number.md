Your nol card number is the **10-digit tag ID**. You need it for an online balance check, and it's the number that identifies your card. This page shows where to find it, which number to use, and what to do when the digits are hard to read.

<div class="note">

**Quick answer:** the 10-digit tag ID is printed on the back of your nol card. Use that number, and nothing else, for a balance check.

</div>

## Where the 10-digit tag ID is printed

Turn your card over. The tag ID is on the back, as a run of 10 digits.

<ol class="steps">
<li>Hold the card in good light and turn it to the back.</li>
<li>Find the 10-digit number. Ignore any other numbers on the card.</li>
<li>Write it down, or copy it digit by digit.</li>
</ol>

<div class="tip">

**Tip:** type the number slowly and check it against the card. One wrong digit gives you the wrong card or no result.

</div>

## Which number to use for a balance check

Use only the 10-digit tag ID. Other numbers on the card are not the balance-check number, so don't enter them on [nol.rta.ae](https://nol.rta.ae/) or in any app.

| Item | Number to use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Silver or Gold card | 10-digit tag ID (back of card) | Standard plastic card, AED 25 |
| Personal (Blue) card | 10-digit tag ID (back of card) | Personalised with name and photo, AED 70 |
| Red ticket | No plastic card to read | Paper ticket, see below |

## Red ticket vs plastic card

A red ticket is paper, not plastic. It's a short-term option for visitors and occasional riders. Plastic cards last much longer and carry a balance you can top up.

| | Plastic card (Silver, Gold, Personal) | Red ticket |
|---|---|---|
| Material | Plastic card | Paper ticket |
| Price | AED 25 (Silver, Gold), AED 70 (Personal) | AED 2 |
| Validity | 5 years | 90 days or 10 trips |
| Use | Pay per trip from your balance | Pay per trip, one type of transport at a time |

For a full comparison, see [nol card types](/nol-card-types) and the [red ticket guide](/nol-red-ticket).

## If the number is worn off or hard to read

Cards wear over time, so the printed number can fade. Try these steps:

<ol class="steps">
<li>Look at the back in good light, and try a few different angles.</li>
<li>Compare the digits with a clear photo of the card, if you took one when it was new.</li>
<li>If you still can't read all 10 digits, call RTA on <strong>800 9090</strong>. Don't guess.</li>
</ol>

<div class="warn">

**Stay safe:** this guide cannot check your balance and never asks for your card number. Enter the tag ID only on rta.ae or nol.rta.ae.

</div>

## Keep a written copy of your tag ID

Write the 10-digit tag ID down and store it away from the card. If the card is lost, damaged or faulty, the number makes it easier to describe the card when you report it. Lost cards can be reported and refunded through the RTA website, Smart Kiosks or the nol Pay app. Read the [lost nol card guide](/lost-nol-card) for the full process.

## What the tag ID is used for

- Checking your balance on [nol.rta.ae](https://nol.rta.ae/) or in the RTA Dubai app.
- Topping up on the RTA website or in the RTA Dubai and S'hail apps.
- Registering an anonymous card, so it can be linked to you. See the [register your nol card guide](/register-nol-card).

## Quick check that the number is right

Before you rely on a balance check, run through these points:

- The number has exactly 10 digits.
- You copied it from the back of the card you're checking, not from another card.
- The balance shown matches what you expect to see. If it doesn't, check each digit again.
- If the check still returns no result, call RTA on 800 9090 instead of trying random numbers.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up digits.** Read the number slowly, and check similar-looking digits twice.
- **Using the PIN.** The SMS PIN is for refunds and managing cards. It is not the tag ID, so don't enter it on a balance check.
- **Copying from a receipt.** Use the number printed on the card itself.
- **Adding spaces or dashes.** Enter the digits as one run of 10.
